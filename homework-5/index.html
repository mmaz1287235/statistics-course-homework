Numerical Integration
and the Normal CDF

Approximating the same area in two different ways, investigating where numerical sampling fails, and computing probabilities under the standard normal curve.

Exercise 1 compares domain partitions with value-level partitions. Exercise 2 compares three algorithms for Φ(x). All calculations run locally in JavaScript; no external plotting or numerical libraries are required.
01

Two views of an integral

What is partitioned, and what is measured?

Riemann: partition the domain

Split [a, b] into n equal intervals, of width Δx = (b − a)/n, and evaluate the function at their midpoints cᵢ.

Rₙ = Σᵢ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹ f(cᵢ) Δx

For bounded functions, Riemann integrability requires upper and lower sums to approach the same limit as the mesh tends to zero. A single sequence of midpoint sums is evidence, not a proof of integrability.

Lebesgue: partition function values

For a nonnegative measurable function, the layer-cake identity represents its integral using the sizes of superlevel sets:

∫ f dλ = ∫₀^∞ λ({x : f(x) ≥ t}) dt

For a bounded function (or a capped version fM = min(f, M)), use a value step h = M/m and the two bounding sums:

Lₘ = h Σₖ₌₁ᵐ λ({fM ≥ kh}) Uₘ = h Σₖ₌₀ᵐ⁻¹ λ({fM > kh})

For functions with positive-measure level sets, the ≥ versus > distinction matters. Both sums bound the integral and their difference is at most h(b − a). Here superlevel measures are obtained analytically, not estimated by Riemann sampling.

03

What a finite grid can miss

Dirichlet and Thomae are different mathematical cases.

Dirichlet's function

D(x) = 1 if x ∈ ℚ; 0 otherwise

Every interval contains rational and irrational points. Every Riemann lower sum is 0 and every upper sum is 1, so the function is not Riemann-integrable.

The rational numbers are countable and have Lebesgue measure zero. Therefore ∫D dλ = 0. Every rational midpoint grid gives 1, while the shifted irrational grid in the table gives 0. Neither outcome can establish a Riemann integral.

Thomae's function

T(p/q) = 1/q (p/q reduced) T(x) = 0 for irrational x

T is continuous at every irrational number and discontinuous at every rational number. Its set of discontinuities has measure zero, so it is Riemann-integrable and its integral is 0.

A rational midpoint grid can still produce nonzero sums. As the mesh decreases, these sums approach 0. Value levels of positive height have zero-measure superlevel sets, giving a Lebesgue integral of 0.

Sampling demonstration

All midpoint fractions (2i + 1)/(2n) are rational and reduced exactly with integer arithmetic. The alternative points (i + √2/2)/n are irrational in exact mathematics. In JavaScript their floating-point representations do not support a reliable rationality test; values on that grid are assigned from the mathematical construction, not inferred from floating-point bits.

04

The standard normal CDF

From a non-elementary antiderivative to practical numerical methods.

φ(x) = exp(−x²/2) / √(2π) Φ(x) = ∫₋∞ˣ φ(t) dt = 1/2 + ∫₀ˣ φ(t) dt

The Gaussian density is normalized to total area 1. Its antiderivative is not expressible using a finite combination of elementary functions. Liouville's theorem on elementary integration gives a criterion for when an elementary antiderivative can exist; applying it to the Gaussian exponential shows why one cannot be constructed. This does not mean the integral is undefined.

erf(z) = (2/√π) ∫₀ᶻ exp(−t²) dt Φ(x) = 1/2 · [1 + erf(x/√2)]

The error function is an exact special-function representation, not an elementary closed form. Its practical evaluation still involves an algorithm. Here, Simpson's rule integrates from 0 to |x|, a series expands the same Gaussian integral, and a five-coefficient approximation evaluates erf. Symmetry Φ(−x) = 1 − Φ(x) is used in each implementation.

05

Conclusions and limits

What the comparisons do, and do not, establish.

Equal integral, different partitions

For every bounded Riemann-integrable example in the lab, the domain-midpoint sums and the value-level lower and upper sums converge to the same integral. This is a theorem about Riemann-integrable functions, not an inference from a finite table. The value-level bracket is certified because exact superlevel measures are used.

Accuracy and computational cost

For smooth functions midpoint Riemann sums often converge quickly; a jump may be particularly favorable when partition boundaries align. Value-level sums always satisfy U − L ≤ M(b − a)/m. The implementations here evaluate one function per midpoint and one superlevel measure per level; the cost is O(n) and O(m), respectively. These counts are not a claim that each measure is equally cheap to derive or evaluate.

Improper integration needs an extra limit

For f(x) = 1/√x, the midpoint method avoids evaluating x = 0 and approximates the improper integral as n grows. The value-level construction uses fM = min(f, M): increasing m at a fixed M converges only to 2 − 1/M. One must also let M → ∞ to reach 2. No finite cap can remove that truncation error.

When point sampling is misleading

Dirichlet's function is not Riemann-integrable despite having a Lebesgue integral. Thomae's function is Riemann-integrable even though every rational sample can be positive. These are exact statements derived from measure and continuity, not conclusions based only on floating-point tests.

Why the CDF matters

Normal CDF evaluations turn standardized scores into probabilities and p-values, including in statistical monitoring and anomaly detection. Accuracy requirements differ: approximate probabilities near the center can be easy to compute, while tiny tail probabilities may require complementary-tail algorithms to avoid cancellation. The implementations on this page are a bounded educational comparison, not a production-grade extreme-tail probability library.
