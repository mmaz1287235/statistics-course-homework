Stochastic Penetration Scores
and Statistical Limits

I simulate how independent penetration scores accumulate across systems. The same experiments show why averages become stable, while properly normalized fluctuations remain random.

Interpretation. Each of the m attackers is represented by one independent trajectory across n systems. The scores are simplified mathematical outcomes, not estimates of real-world compromise probabilities.
01

The stochastic model

Two ways of assigning a score to each attempted system.

Bernoulli increments

For an attacker, Xᵢ is 1 when an attempt succeeds and 0 otherwise. Each attempt succeeds independently with probability p.

P(Xᵢ = 1) = p
E[Xᵢ] = p
Var(Xᵢ) = p(1 − p)

This is a count of successful attempts under an independent, constant-probability assumption.

Rademacher increments

Each attempt changes a signed score by +1 or −1, each with probability 1/2. The resulting trajectory is a symmetric random walk.

P(Xᵢ = +1) = P(Xᵢ = −1) = 1/2
E[Xᵢ] = 0
Var(Xᵢ) = 1

A negative total is a net negative score, not a negative number of compromised systems.

What changes under normalization?

Sₙ = Σᵢ Xᵢnμnσ²Raw fluctuations grow

Here μ and σ² are the mean and variance of an individual increment. Unlike the zero-mean Rademacher case, uncentered Bernoulli scores Sₙ/√n have a mean that grows as p√n.

04

Law of Large Numbers

The probability of a substantial deviation from μ becomes smaller as n grows.

Convergence in probability

For any fixed ε > 0, the weak law states that P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) tends to zero as n tends to infinity.

P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) ≤ σ² / (n ε²)

The inequality is Chebyshev's upper bound. It need not be close to the observed probability, particularly at small n.

An observed frequency of 0 means that none of the simulated attackers crossed the chosen threshold. It does not imply that the theoretical probability is zero. Chebyshev gives an upper bound, not an estimate of the exact probability.

05

Central Limit Theorem

In the symmetric case, the distribution of square-root-normalized scores approaches a normal shape.

The target distribution

For independent Rademacher increments, the individual score has mean zero and variance one. Therefore:

Sₙ / √n ⇒ N(0, 1)

For Bernoulli increments with 0 < p < 1, the corresponding standardization instead subtracts np and divides by √(np(1 − p)). Without centering, Sₙ/√n is not expected to converge to N(0, 1): its mean grows like p√n, even though its variance stays fixed.

Empirical distribution and standard normal density

Observed histogram densityStandard normal density

For each selected n, the score remains discrete. The normal curve approximates its distribution as n increases; it does not make individual scores continuous. At small n, the discrete support can also make the observed proportion within one standard deviation differ noticeably from the normal reference.

06

Interpretation and limitations

What the simulation tells us, and what it does not.

System size and the attackers

For Bernoulli scores, a single attacker accumulates about np successes, while the fraction of successful attempts settles near p. In the symmetric model the total wanders in both directions; its typical magnitude grows on the order of √n even though the average tends toward zero.

Increasing m gives more trajectories and usually smoother histograms. It does not change the expectation or variance of one attacker's score. If instead we summed scores across all attackers, that would be a different aggregate quantity with an extra factor of m in its expectation and variance.

Assumptions and limitations

The experiments assume independent attackers, independent system outcomes and identical probabilities across attempts. Real penetration attempts may share vulnerabilities, depend on previous compromises or react to defensive controls. In those situations the simple i.i.d. model can fail.

Finite experiments only illustrate LLN and CLT. A good histogram fit or a small observed deviation probability is not a proof of convergence. The mathematical results come from the assumptions, while the simulation helps make their implications visible.
