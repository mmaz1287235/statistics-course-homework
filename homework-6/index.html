Statistical
Independence

From paired categorical observations to a contingency table, expected frequencies, and a test for association.

This page uses two explicitly synthetic cybersecurity datasets. Both examples are constructed for teaching purposes and do not describe observed incidents. All counts, conditional frequencies, expected frequencies, and test results are recomputed from individual records in the browser.
01

Independence in probability and statistics

The population property and the sample-based test answer different questions.

Probability independence

For categorical random variables X and F, independence means that every joint probability factorizes into the product of its marginals:

P(X = xᵢ, F = fⱼ) = P(X = xᵢ) P(F = fⱼ)

Equivalently, P(F = fⱼ | X = xᵢ) = P(F = fⱼ) whenever P(X = xᵢ) > 0. This is a property of the underlying joint distribution, not a statement proved by matching a finite table.

Statistical hypothesis

For a random sample of n independent paired observations, Pearson's chi-square test assesses

H₀: X and F are independent H₁: X and F are associated

With r observed categories of X and c of F, the null reference distribution is approximately chi-square with (r − 1)(c − 1) degrees of freedom when its assumptions are appropriate.

What the frequencies represent

Observed cell count: Oᵢⱼ Joint relative frequency: Oᵢⱼ / n Row conditional frequency: Oᵢⱼ / Oᵢ· Column conditional frequency: Oᵢⱼ / O·ⱼ Marginal relative frequencies: Oᵢ· / n, O·ⱼ / n

These are empirical frequencies. They estimate probabilities but generally do not match the population probabilities exactly.

Bivariate dataset

Every record is a pair (X, F); the tables are constructed by counting these pairs.

X is the recorded attack vector (Phishing, Credential abuse, Malware). F is the investigation outcome (Blocked, Contained, Compromised). Each row represents one independent hypothetical incident. Labels are analytical categories, not claims about real-world attack probabilities.

04

Expected frequencies and the chi-square test

Compare the observed table with the independence model.

Expected counts under H₀

Eᵢⱼ = (Oᵢ· × O·ⱼ) / n

The independence model preserves the observed row and column totals. It does not assert that all cells should have the same size.

Pearson statistic

χ² = Σᵢ Σⱼ (Oᵢⱼ − Eᵢⱼ)² / Eᵢⱼ df = (r − 1)(c − 1)

A large statistic is evidence against H₀. Its upper-tail probability is p = P(χ²df ≥ χ²observed), calculated numerically.

Contributions to χ² by cell

Cramér's V: strength of sample association

V = √[χ² / (n × min(r − 1, c − 1))]

V ranges from 0 to 1 for a contingency table; larger values indicate stronger discrepancy from independence. It summarizes association rather than causal influence, and its interpretation depends on the table and context.

05

Conclusions and limitations

Interpret a test decision without confusing association with causation.

Interpretation of the current dataset

A statistical test is not proof of independence

Failure to reject H₀ means the data do not provide sufficient evidence of association at the chosen significance level. It does not establish independence. A significant result means the observed discrepancy is unlikely under the null model, subject to the model and sampling assumptions.

Assumptions and scope

Observations should be independent, categories should be mutually exclusive and consistently recorded, and expected counts should be sufficiently large for the chi-square approximation. This page flags any expected count below 5 as a conservative warning; that threshold is a practical rule of thumb, not a universal theorem. If the conditions do not hold, a different method or better data may be appropriate.

Association is not causation

Even if attack vector and outcome are associated in incident data, the table alone cannot establish that one variable causes the other. Severity, exposure, detection mechanisms, reporting procedures, and selection effects might influence both. The datasets on this page are simulated illustrations: they support computations, not empirical conclusions about cybersecurity.

Reproducibility and interpretation

The two example datasets are deterministic and are formed by expanding explicitly documented count matrices into (X, F) records. Editing the rows recalculates every displayed result. This is a demonstration of methods, not a random-sampling study; p-values are descriptive exercises under the stated sampling model, not evidence about a real population.
